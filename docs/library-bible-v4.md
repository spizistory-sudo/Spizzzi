## 0. Scope — the three Spizzzy bands (all LIVE)

| Band | Format | Form | Length | Rubric |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2–4** | Board / rhyme book | Read-aloud, rhyme-forward | ~50–350 words | §11R (live, v4) |
| **4–6** | Picture book | Read-aloud prose, 12–16 spreads | 400–750 words | §10R (live) |
| **6–8** | Early chapter book | Independent, short illustrated chapters | 2,500–10,000 words | §12R (live, v3) |

Middle grade (8–12) is out of scope. Comics/graphic-novel is a possible future track.

**Rubric selection (critical):** the Checker reads the book's `age_band` and applies THAT
band's rubric only — 2–4 → §11R, 4–6 → §10R, 6–8 → §12R. It never applies one band's gates to
another band's book, and it always records which band rubric it applied.

**Kids read up:** protagonist age sits at/above the top of the band (2–4 → ~4; 4–6 → ~6–7;
6–8 → ~8–9). For 2–4, the "protagonist" is often the child listener themselves or a stand-in
(a toddler, a small animal), so "read up" here means ~3–4, relatable as a slightly older self.

## 11. The 2–4 board/rhyme book — band spec *(LIVE, v4)*

This is the lap book — performed by an adult to a very young child, often at bedtime, over and
over. It is the band where **sound comes before sense**: the point is the music of language,
the comfort of repetition, and the ritual, more than plot.

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Reader | Baby/toddler; an adult reads/performs it aloud |
| "Protagonist" | The child stand-in — a toddler, a small animal, or "you" |
| Form | **Rhyme-forward.** Fully rhymed, or clean simple prose with strong repetition. **Never half-rhymed, never broken meter, never mixed.** |
| Length | **~50–350 words** |
| Architecture | 10–16 spreads, **very few words per spread** (often one line or couplet) |
| Structure | One feeling / one moment / one routine: feeling → tiny journey → comfort |
| Language | Tiny vocabulary; one idea per line; concrete, nameable things |
| Repetition | Heavy and deliberate — a refrain the child can **finish** ("finish the line" is the band's signature behavior) |
| Sound | Rhythm, bounce, onomatopoeia, sounds to make together |
| Themes | Routines, bedtime, family, animals, the body, naming the world, first feelings |
| Ending | **Always** safe, soothed, reassured. No unresolved worry at this age |

**On rhyme:** this is the band where rhyme belongs — rhyme and rhythm awareness are a
recognized predictor of how easily a child learns to read, and the beat is doing developmental
work. But that raises the bar, it doesn't lower it: **bad rhyme is worse than no rhyme.** A
forced rhyme, a wrong stress, or a near-rhyme is felt immediately by the adult reading aloud
and ruins the ritual. If clean rhyme can't be achieved for a beat, switch that book to simple
repetitive prose rather than limping through broken verse.

## 11R. The 2–4 Checker rubric *(LIVE, v4)*

Apply this rubric — not §10R or §12R — to any book with `age_band: 2–4`.

**Hard gates (⛔ — must pass):**
- **Length** — ~50–350 words; very few words per spread (10–16 spreads).
- **Form integrity** — fully rhymed OR clean repetitive prose; **never mixed, never
  half-rhymed.**
- **METER & RHYME GATE (the band's strictest gate)** — if rhymed: every rhyme is a true rhyme
  (matching final stressed sounds, not near-rhyme); the meter is consistent across the whole
  book (scan every line — the stress pattern must hold); no line is padded or inverted to force
  a rhyme; the book reads aloud with an unbroken bounce. **A single forced rhyme or metrical
  stumble fails this gate.** When uncertain, fail and flag for human ear — do not pass
  questionable meter.
- **Refrain / repetition** — there is a repeated, finishable line or pattern the child can join.
- **Value/feeling shown, not stated** — no moral tag; at this age the "value" is usually a
  feeling or a comfort, carried by the moment, not explained.
- **Age & safety** — tiny vocabulary; concrete nameable things; nothing frightening; the
  landing is safe and soothed.
- **Coherence** — the moment/routine holds together start to finish; 10–16 spreads; each spread
  has text (often one line) + illustration note.
- **Character sheet completeness** — locked features + palette (consistency still matters).
- **Concrete language** — nameable, sensory, physical; no abstraction.

**Scored (0–5 each):**
- **Read-aloud music (weighted highest at this age)** — bounce, rhythm, joy in the mouth. This
  is the whole game for 2–4.
- **Comfort / warmth** — does the landing soothe? Is it a good bedtime book?
- **Participation** — does the refrain invite the child to join and finish lines?
- **Delight** — a giggle, a surprise, a sound to make together.
- **Specificity & freshness** — its own book, not a generic "goodnight everything" template.
- **Dual-audience layer** — does the adult enjoy the hundredth reading?
- **Illustration partnership** — clear, warm, single-focus images a toddler can read.

**Threshold:** all hard gates pass (the meter gate especially), average ≥ 3.5, no single score
below 3.

**Human-ear note (important):** 2–4 is the band where the Checker is least reliable, because
grading meter and true rhyme by model is genuinely hard. Treat a 2–4 "ready" as *provisionally*
ready — the human review step should always read it aloud before approving. When the Checker is
unsure about meter, it should escalate rather than pass.
